## Proposed Route Groupings

---

### Route A: "Earn the right to make the exceptional claim"

**Route title:** Earn the right to make the exceptional claim  
**Description:** Before Cafe Barra can credibly position itself as an exceptional,
verifiable partner, the internal operations must be reliable enough to back that
claim without overpromising. This route builds the operational and process
foundation that makes the exceptional positioning earned rather than asserted.

**State at migration:** Diagnose (internal evidence present; no customer validation yet)

#### Legs under Route A

| Leg # | Existing card | Rationale |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| A1 | Make margin tradeoffs visible before pricing changes (fix) | Financial reliability is a precondition for confident pricing conversations with partners; without a margin model, pricing is reactive and erodes trust |
| A2 | Reduce reorder friction caused by unclear supplier terms (fix) | Supply reliability is the operational promise Cafe Barra must be able to keep; unclear terms create the exact stock-out risk that makes partners lose confidence |
| A3 | Reduce stock-out risk before manual counts fail (improve) | Direct manifestation of operational reliability; this is what failing on the partner promise looks like in practice |
| A4 | Shift preparation quality from manager-dependent to system-supported (improve) | Cafe-side quality consistency must hold when the owner isn't present; system-level quality is the prerequisite for any scalable partner relationship |

**Why these four belong together:** All four address the same underlying risk —
that Cafe Barra's operational reality doesn't yet match its positioning claims. A
partner who discovers stock-outs, pricing confusion, or inconsistent preparation
quality after choosing Cafe Barra will not renew. These legs create the floor.

#### Rejected alternatives for Route A
1. **"Hire for capacity before systematizing"** — *Rejected because:* Preparation
   quality and financial clarity are currently manager-dependent. Scaling headcount
   before systematizing would scale the inconsistency, not resolve it. Premature
   growth would require a painful reset and would damage partner relationships in
   the interim.
2. **"Accept operational fragility as a startup reality and focus on partner
   acquisition"** — *Rejected because:* The positioning as a high-trust, verifiable
   quality partner requires operational reliability as evidence. A single high-profile
   stock-out or quality failure with an early partner could permanently damage the
   relationship and the referral chain that early partners represent.

#### What would have to be true (Route A)
1. A margin model can be built from existing POS and invoice data without external
   tooling investment
2. Supplier terms can be clarified through direct negotiation, not contract replacement
3. The POS system has enough inventory capability to support par-level alerting
4. Preparation quality variance is addressable through documentation, not hiring
5. These four operational improvements can be sequenced without each depending on
   the others completing first

### Route B: "Make the Barra Process visible and transferable"

**Route title:** Make the Barra Process visible and transferable  
**Description:** The Barra Process — five roasting templates, seasonal origin
adaptation, the 3-sample protocol — currently lives in instinct and careful notes.
This route converts that expertise into something partners can observe, verify, and
trust before commitment. It is also the route that validates whether the "exceptional
vs really good" positioning claim holds under direct comparison.

**State at migration:** Diagnose (internal documentation present; external validation missing)

#### Legs under Route B

| Leg # | Existing card | Rationale |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| B1 | Externalize one Barra roasting template into observable, partner-communicable criteria (improve) | The foundation leg: if the quality ceiling can be documented without losing precision, all subsequent proof artifacts become possible; if it can't, the positioning has a structural ceiling |
| B2 | Design the seasonal origin transition so partner cafes experience it as a methodology feature, not a supply disruption (fix) | The seasonal model is Cafe Barra's deliberate quality approach — partners must experience it as a feature, not a failure; this communication design leg converts the Barra Process from internal philosophy to external proof |
| B3 | Build a seasonal consistency signal partner cafes can observe independently (create) | Creates the verifiable artifact that lets prospects evaluate quality before commitment — the "trust but verify" entry point for new partners |
| B4 | Test whether the exceptional positioning holds under direct comparison with premium craft alternatives (create) | The positioning claim needs to survive direct comparison with Blue Bottle and Stumptown from a prospect who has already used them; this test either confirms the claim or forces a revision before it creates expectation gaps |

**Why these four belong together:** All four move the Barra Process from
"trust us, we're exceptional" toward "here is evidence you can verify yourself."
B1 is the prerequisite (can the process be externalized at all?); B2 converts
the process into a communication artifact; B3 creates an independent proof
object; B4 stress-tests the positioning claim against live comparison. The legs
are sequentially dependent in spirit but can progress in parallel.

#### Rejected alternatives for Route B
1. **"Build a brand/marketing campaign around the exceptional claim before
   it's verifiable"** — *Rejected because:* The alternatives analysis shows that
   the relevant buyers (specialty store owners) are sophisticated — they will
   compare, not defer. Marketing that gets ahead of verifiable proof would set
   expectations the process can't yet meet for prospects who haven't been through
   it. Proof first, then amplification.
2. **"License or acquire an established craft brand rather than building
   Barra's own verifiable identity"** — *Rejected because:* The Barra Process
   is the competitive moat. Acquiring a brand would replace Cafe Barra's
   differentiated methodology with someone else's — trading the most defensible
   asset for a shortcut that doesn't compound.
3. **"Delay proof-building until more partners are acquired"** — *Rejected
   because:* The proof is what enables acquisition of the next partner. Waiting
   for more partners to build proof creates a catch-22; the seasonal consistency
   signal and comparison test are how the first selective partners get closed.

#### What would have to be true (Route B)
1. At least one Barra roasting template can be written in observable, non-instinct
   criteria without losing the quality ceiling
2. Partner cafes will value a documented consistency artifact — i.e., they care
   enough about quality verification to use it
3. The seasonal origin model is communicable in a one-page brief that a partner
   reads as intentional methodology, not supplier inconsistency
4. At least one prospective partner using Blue Bottle or Stumptown can be engaged
   for a blind comparison test
5. The "exceptional vs really good" distinction is perceptible to prospects, not
   just to Cafe Barra — the taste difference survives direct side-by-side evaluation

### Route C: "Win the right partners through evidence, not pitch"

**Route title:** Win the right partners through evidence, not pitch  
**Description:** The partner pipeline currently relies on a full structured interview
as the only filter, and on assertion rather than demonstrated proof to convert
prospects. This route makes the pipeline more selective at the top (pre-qualification)
and more evidence-based at the bottom (proof changes repeat behavior). Route C is
the thinnest route at this stage — two legs — and will grow as more partner acquisition
insights emerge.

**State at migration:** Outside view (legs are early-stage tests, no evidence yet)

#### Legs under Route C

| Leg # | Existing card | Rationale |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| C1 | Add a lightweight pre-qualification tier before the full partner interview (fix) | Protects the full interview process for accounts already showing alignment signals; the 8-criteria framework is currently used as a screen when it was designed as an evaluation instrument |
| C2 | Test whether operational proof changes repeat purchasing confidence (create) | The hypothesis that documented operational proof changes partner behavior needs to be tested directly; this leg is a structured learning exercise, not an acquisition motion |

**Why these two belong together:** Both address the same question: does evidence
change partner behavior? C1 asks whether pre-qualification signals predict
alignment (upstream). C2 asks whether proof artifacts change purchasing confidence
(downstream). Together they build a data-informed picture of what actually moves
partners through the pipeline.

**Note on thinness:** Route C has two legs at this stage. This is intentional —
the partner acquisition picture will become clearer as Routes A and B progress.
New legs should be added to Route C as evidence accumulates about what actually
moves selective partners. Do not force artificial legs here.

#### Rejected alternatives for Route C
1. **"Scale outbound partner prospecting before the proof artifacts are ready"** —
   *Rejected because:* Outbound to prospects who can't yet be converted by evidence
   wastes evaluation capacity and may generate accounts that are misaligned; the
   Partner Selection Framework explicitly requires relationship depth the existing
   process is designed to build.

#### What would have to be true (Route C)
1. Pre-qualification signals (how an inquiry arrives, what it leads with) are
   observable enough to score before a full interview
2. The written async screen can correctly identify misaligned accounts before
   the interview stage
3. Documented operational proof (case evidence from existing partners) is
   meaningful to prospective partners, not dismissed as self-reported marketing
4. The prospective partner who sees proof converts at a materially higher rate
   than one who receives only the positioning narrative
