## Section 1: Strategy Cascade

### Reconstructed (frameworks_used: {ODI, strategy_cascade, reconstructed_prior})

**Winning Aspiration:**
> Be the most trusted specialty coffee sourcing and roast development partner for independent cafe
> operators who want to build a distinctive offering.

**Where to Play:**
> Independent cafes and hospitality venues with 1-5 locations who want to differentiate on coffee
> quality. Not commodity buyers or chains. Operators who compete on customer experience and want
> coffee to be a meaningful part of it.

**How to Win:**
> Win by combining superior roast profile development support with operational reliability —
> making it easier for cafe owners to build a distinctive offering they can actually sustain.
> Supplier switching must remain easy enough that proof gaps change behavior. Current proof is
> mostly public signals — confidence should stay provisional until direct customer evidence
> accumulates.

**Assessment:** Directionally correct on market (B2B, independent cafes, 1-5 locations). Correctly
identifies roast development support + operational reliability as the differentiator. The caveat
"confidence should stay provisional" reflects reconstruction uncertainty, not Cafe Barra's own
confidence. Evidence should remove this hedging.

---

### Evidence Signal (from active files)

**From Strategic Framework Final:**
- Job executor: "cafe owners and specialty retailers in LA who take their coffee program
  seriously and are frustrated that their current roaster doesn't"
- Three core jobs documented: (1) Build a distinctive specialty coffee identity, (2) Achieve
  consistent quality without owning the roasting infrastructure, (3) Grow the business without
  sacrificing what makes the coffee special
- Positioning via April Dunford: "the roast development partner for independent cafe owners who
  want to build a coffee program they're proud of"
- Unit economics discussed: per-bag margin model, volume thresholds for partnership depth

**From Positioning (Dunford framework):**
- Competitive alternatives table: Commodity distributors (no differentiation), Premium single-origin
  direct-trade (inconsistent supply), In-house roasting (capital drain)
- Codified partnership model as the differentiator
- "The roast partner for cafes that take quality seriously"

**From Alternatives Analysis:**
- "The gap between really good and exceptional is qualitatively different — it's not a bigger
  version of the same thing, it's a different category of experience entirely"
- Alternative quality ceilings mapped: Commodity = "good enough", Local artisan = "really good
  (inconsistently)", In-house = "variable", Cafe Barra = "exceptional"

**From The Barra Process:**
- 5 roasting templates, matched to bean origin/density characteristics
- Seasonal sourcing (buy for a season, test and adapt each batch)
- Consistency defined as "consistent quality" not "same flavor profile every season"
- "Science meets art meets commitment" — the process promise

**From Partner Selection Framework:**
- 8-criteria scorecard: Brand Intent, Quality Standards, Training Culture, Partnership
  Orientation, Operational Discipline, Volume Viability, Communication, Strategic Alignment
- Decline criteria: prioritizes price over quality, refuses staff training, treats suppliers as
  interchangeable
- Partner philosophy: "Coffee should make a cafe more distinctive and desirable"

**Evidence-grounded Winning Aspiration (what it should be):**
> Be the specialty roast development partner for independent cafe owners who want their coffee to
> set them apart — providing seasonal sourcing, distinctive roast profiles, and the operational
> partnership support that chains and commodity distributors can't match.

**Key corrections to reconstructed version:**
- Drop hedging language ("confidence should stay provisional") — the evidence supports this
  strongly
- Add seasonal sourcing model specificity
- Add the partner selection discipline (not every cafe is a fit)
- LA geography is explicit in the files; reconstructed version omitted it

## Section 2: Positioning Canvas

### Reconstructed (frameworks_used: {ODI, strategy_cascade, public_baseline, reconstructed_prior})

**Competitive Alternatives:**
1. Commodity wholesale distributors — Lower cost but no roast development support
2. Premium direct-trade single-origin roasters — High quality but inconsistent supply
3. In-house roasting — Maximum control but capital/expertise/time drain

**Value for Customer:**
> We help independent cafe owners build a distinctive specialty coffee offering — one where
> roast profile, supplier relationships, and product quality are strong enough to become the
> reason customers choose them.

**Best Fit Customers:**
> Cafe owners and venue operators who want their coffee to be a differentiator, not a commodity.
> Typically 1-5 location independents with a clear vision for the customer experience.

**Market Category:** Specialty coffee roasting and B2B sourcing

**Current Tagline:** "Specialty coffee that sets your establishment apart."

**Proposed Tagline:** "The roast partner for cafes that take quality seriously."

**Assessment:** Competitive alternatives match exactly what the evidence documents. Value
proposition is directionally right but missing the specificity of the Barra Process (the
methodology itself is a differentiator). Best-fit customers are correctly identified. Taglines
match the evidence file language closely (proposed tagline appears verbatim in Strategic Framework).

  In-house = "variable", Barra = "exceptional (consistent)"
- "Good enough is a ceiling, not a floor — it guarantees your cafe will never be memorable"

**From The Barra Process (unique attribute):**

**From Partner Selection Framework:**

- This is NOT in the reconstructed positioning canvas — missing key differentiator

**Evidence-grounded corrections:**
1. Add "Barra Roast Method" / 5-template roasting methodology as unique attribute
2. Add partner qualification as unique attribute (systematic fit vs. volume)
3. Proposed tagline "The roast partner for cafes that take quality seriously" — already matches
   evidence exactly; keep

   alternatives)

### Evidence Signal (customer job outcomes from files)

**From Strategic Framework Final — documented customer jobs for cafe owners:**
1. Minimize time evaluating whether a roaster's philosophy matches theirs
2. Increase confidence that roast quality will remain consistent across seasonal sourcing shifts
3. Minimize effort required to communicate quality standards to baristas using the new coffee
4. Reduce the risk of inconsistency when the roaster adapts to a new origin
5. Increase confidence that the partnership approach will sustain when volume grows

**From Barra Process — specific process-step needs:**
1. Minimize effort in dialing in a new origin batch (5 templates reduce this)
2. Increase consistency of roast quality across different bean densities and origins
3. Reduce the time to determine if a new bean meets quality standards (3 sample roasts)

**From Partner Selection Framework — cafe owner evaluation needs:**
1. Minimize time spent evaluating whether a new roaster fits the cafe's quality philosophy
2. Reduce risk of partnering with a roaster who treats the relationship as transactional
3. Increase confidence that staff will be supported in learning the new coffee program

**From Alternatives Analysis — unmet needs from alternatives:**
1. Get exceptional rather than "really good" consistency without owning roasting infrastructure
2. Reduce the unpredictability of seasonal variation in craft roaster supply
3. Eliminate the capital and attention cost of in-house roasting while maintaining quality ceiling

**What evidence-derived needs should anchor to:**
| Journey Step | Evidence-Based Need Category |
|---|---|
| Step 1: Define desired progress | Clarity on what "exceptional" coffee means for their brand |
| Step 2: Locate viable options | Evaluating roaster quality and consistency signals |
| Step 3: Identify best fit | Assessing methodology match and partnership philosophy |
| Step 4: Confirm readiness | Operational and financial viability check |
| Step 5: Perform core task | Transitioning to new roaster, training staff |
| Step 6: Monitor results | Tracking quality, customer response, consistency |
| Step 7: Adjust approach | Handling seasonal variation, new origins |
| Step 8: Conclude and learn | Evaluating partnership depth, scaling decision |

## Section 4: Routes

### Reconstructed (frameworks_used with reconstructed_prior)

1. **Create**: "Test whether operational proof changes repeat purchasing confidence" (pts: 9)
2. **Fix**: "Make margin tradeoffs visible before pricing changes" (pts: 8)

4. **Improve**: "Reduce stock-out risk before manual counts fail" (pts: 7)
5. **Improve**: "Shift preparation quality from manager-dependent to system-supported" (pts: 6)

**Assessment:** These routes are operationally reasonable for a food service B2B business, but
they're generic — they don't reflect Cafe Barra's specific positioning (roast development, the
Barra Process, partner qualification). Route #1 is the only one that references the correct
concept (operational proof → repeat purchasing). Routes 2-5 feel like supply chain
management for a commodity distributor, not a craft roast development partner.

---

### Evidence-Grounded Routes (what they should be)

Based on evidence files:

1. **Create**: Build proof that the Barra Roast Method produces measurably better seasonal
   consistency than alternatives — making the methodology the purchase justification, not just
   the story.
   - Evidence: Alternatives analysis shows "inconsistency" is the #1 weakness of craft alternatives
   - Source: comp-alt + unique-attr files

2. **Improve**: Systematize the partner discovery interview so it reduces time-to-fit evaluation
   without losing the qualitative judgment Cafe Barra's model depends on.
   - Evidence: Partner Selection Framework has the 8-category scorecard; interview guide exists
   - Source: referral-map file

3. **Fix**: Close the gap between roast methodology documentation and what cafe staff can
   actually learn — the Barra Process requires contextual transfer that current onboarding
   doesn't support.
   - Evidence: Training Culture is one of the 8 partner selection criteria; staff training cited
     as key differentiator in partner philosophy
   - Source: referral-map + unique-attr files

4. **Improve**: Formalize seasonal origin transition communications so cafes experience quality
   adaptation as a partnership feature, not a supply disruption.
   - Evidence: Barra Process describes seasonal adaptation; this is a positioning feature but
     needs operational communication design to be experienced that way
   - Source: unique-attr file

5. **Create**: Develop a measurable quality consistency signal (e.g., barista dialing check,
   taste calibration) that cafe partners can track independently — creating a data layer that
   reinforces the partnership value.
   - Evidence: Competitor weakness is inconsistency; partner selection criteria include Quality
     Standards and Operational Discipline — feedback loop missing
   - Source: comp-alt + referral-map files
